Short Essay Questions

1. A 34-year-old woman with known type 1 diabetes mellitus was admitted following a 'blackout' while driving. She had recently increased her insulin dose and missed two meals.

Model Answers

a) Detection: Hypoglycemia. The plasma glucose is 1.5 mmol/L, significantly below the 5.5-11.1 mmol/L range. (1 Mark)

b) Cause: 1. Increased insulin dose (exogenous hyperinsulinemia) and 2. Starvation/missed meals, leading to excessive glucose uptake by tissues without replacement. (2 Marks)

c) Complications: 1. Neurological dysfunction (Neuroglycopenia/low ATP in brain), 2. Ketoacidosis (accumulation of ketone bodies), and 3. Respiratory distress or weight loss. (3 Marks)

d) Emergency Treatment: Immediate administration of oral glucose or intravenous dextrose/glucagon to raise blood sugar levels. (2 Marks)

e) Advice: Adjust insulin dosage according to meal frequency and ensure insulin is taken 30 minutes before a meal. (2 Marks)

2. A 24-year-old woman presented in a coma. Glucose: 35 mmol/L, pH: 7.10, Bicarbonate: 10 mmol/L, and Urine positive for ketones.

Model Answers

a) Diagnosis: Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) / Hyperglycemic Metabolic Acidosis. (1 Mark)

b) Associated Condition: Diabetes Mellitus Type 1. (1 Mark)

c) Other Findings: 1. Hyperkalemia (Elevated Potassium), 2. Hyponatremia (Low Sodium), and 3. Hypocapnia/Hyperventilation (Reduced partial pressure of CO₂). (3 Marks)

d) Pathophysiology: Reduced insulin leads to low glucose uptake and ATP production. This triggers lipolysis (fat breakdown) for energy, resulting in excessive ketone body production and metabolic acidosis. (3 Marks)

3. A 46-year-old male presents with severe right big toe pain, redness, and swelling. Synovial fluid shows negatively birefringent rod-shaped crystals.

Model Answers

a) Diagnosis: Gouty Arthritis. (2 Marks)

b) Definitive Tests: 1. Serum Uric Acid measurement and 2. Synovial Fluid Examination for monosodium urate crystals. (2 Marks)

c) Biochemical Basis: Increased production of uric acid from purine catabolism. The enzyme Xanthine Oxidase converts hypoxanthine/guanine to xanthine and then to uric acid. (3 Marks)

d) Interventions: 1. NSAIDs (e.g., Betamethasone) to reduce inflammation/pain. 2. Allopurinol (Xanthine Oxidase inhibitor) to reduce uric acid synthesis. (2 Marks)

4. Discuss the biosynthesis and regulation of Cholesterol in human tissues.

Model Answers

a) Biosynthesis Steps: 1. Condensation of 3 Acetyl CoA to form HMG-CoA. 2. HMG-CoA to Mevalonate. 3. Formation of Isoprenoid units. 4. Cyclization of Squalene to Cholesterol. (4 Marks)

b) Regulatory Enzyme: HMG-CoA Reductase. (2 Marks)

c) Regulation Mechanisms: 1. Sterol Dependent: SREBP stimulates gene expression when cholesterol is low. 2. Hormonal: Insulin activates (dephosphorylation) and Glucagon inhibits HMG-CoA reductase. 3. Allosteric: Feedback inhibition by high cholesterol levels. 4. Statins: Drugs like Lovastatin competitively inhibit the enzyme. (4 Marks)

1. A 34-year-old woman with type 1 diabetes was admitted following a 'blackout' while driving. She recently increased her insulin dose due to 'the flu' and missed two meals.

a) What can you detect from the Laboratory results? (1 Mark)

Answer: Hypoglycemia (Plasma glucose is 1.5 mmol/L, well below the reference range of 5.5-11.1 mmol/L).

b) What is the cause of the condition in (a)? (2 Marks)

Answer: 1. Increased insulin dosage (exogenous hyperinsulinemia) and 2. Starvation/fasting (missed two meals), leading to excessive glucose uptake and insufficient glucose intake.

c) State three (3) complications of the condition mentioned in (a)? (3 Marks)

Answer: 1. Neurological dysfunction (Neuroglycopenia due to low ATP in brain tissue), 2. Ketoacidosis (excess ketone bodies in blood), and 3. Weight loss/Respiratory distress.

d) What is the treatment in emergencies? (2 Marks)

Answer: Immediate administration of oral glucose (if conscious) or intravenous dextrose/glucagon (if unconscious).

e) What advice can you give to the patient? (2 Marks)

Answer: Adjust insulin dose according to meal intake and take insulin supplements 30 minutes before a meal to ensure proper glucose regulation.

2. A 24-year-old woman presented in a coma. Glucose: 35 mmol/L, pH: 7.10, Bicarbonate: 10 mmol/L, Urine positive for ketones.

a) What is the diagnosis? (1 Mark)

Answer: Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) / Hyperglycemic Metabolic Acidosis.

b) In which condition will the disease in (a) be presented in? (1 Mark)

Answer: Diabetes Mellitus Type 1.

c) What three (3) other conditions can be detected from the biochemical results? (3 Marks)

Answer: 1. Hyperkalemia (Elevated Potassium), 2. Hyponatremia (Low Sodium), and 3. Hyperventilation (Reduced partial pressure of CO₂).

d) Explain briefly how the condition mentioned in (b) is brought about (3 Marks)

Answer: Reduced insulin levels lead to reduced glucose uptake and ATP production. The body compensates by initiating lipolysis (fat breakdown) to produce energy, resulting in an accumulation of ketone bodies and metabolic acidosis.

3. A 46-year-old male presents with severe right toe pain, redness, and swelling. Synovial fluid shows negatively birefringent rod-shaped crystals.

a) What is the tentative diagnosis? (2 Marks)

Answer: Gouty Arthritis.

b) What laboratory tests would you carry out to arrive at the definitive diagnosis? (2 Marks)

Answer: 1. Measurement of Serum Uric Acid levels and 2. Synovial Fluid Analysis for the presence of monosodium urate crystals.

c) What is the biochemical basis of the condition in (a)? (3 Marks)

Answer: Increased production of uric acid from purine catabolism. The enzyme Xanthine Oxidase catalyzes the conversion of hypoxanthine to xanthine and then to uric acid. Overproduction or under-excretion leads to hyperuricemia.

d) List the interventions that you would suggest as a Medical Doctor in charge (2 Marks)

Answer: 1. Administer NSAIDs/Beta-methasone to reduce inflammation and pain. 2. Administer Allopurinol (Xanthine Oxidase inhibitor) to reduce uric acid synthesis.

4. Cholesterol is synthesized by virtually all tissues in humans.

a) In four (4) steps, outline the biosynthesis of cholesterol (4 Marks)

Answer: 1. Condensation of 3 Acetyl CoA molecules to form HMG-CoA. 2. Conversion of HMG-CoA to Mevalonate. 3. Formation of Isoprenoid units. 4. Cyclization of Squalene to Cholesterol.

b) What is the regulatory enzyme in the cholesterol biosynthetic pathway? (2 Marks)

Answer: HMG-CoA Reductase.

c) List and explain four mechanisms that exist in the regulation of cholesterol synthesis (4 Marks)

Answer: 1. Sterol Dependent Regulation: SREBP protein stimulates HMG-CoA Reductase gene expression when cholesterol levels are low. 2. Hormonal Regulation: Insulin activates HMG-CoA reductase (via dephosphorylation), while Glucagon/Epinephrine inhibit it. 3. Allosteric Regulation: High cholesterol levels act as a feedback inhibitor of the enzyme. 4. Pharmacological (Statins): Drugs like Lovastatin competitively inhibit HMG-CoA Reductase.

Question 1 [10 Marks Total]

a) What can you detect from the Laboratory results? (1 Mark)

Answer: Hypoglycemia. The plasma glucose level is 1.5 mmol/L, which is significantly below the normal range of 5.5-11.1 mmol/L.

b) What is the cause of the condition in (a)? (2 Marks)

Answer: The condition was caused by 1) an excessive insulin dose (relative to needs) and 2) inadequate caloric intake (missed meals). Insulin promotes glucose uptake into cells, and without food intake, blood glucose drops dangerously low.

c) State three (3) complications of the condition mentioned in (a)? (3 Marks)

Answer:

  • Neuroglycopenia: Brain dysfunction due to lack of glucose (confusion, blackouts, seizures).
  • Permanent Brain Damage: Prolonged severe hypoglycemia can lead to neuronal death.
  • Rebound Hyperglycemia (Somogyi Effect): Counter-regulatory hormones trigger a glucose spike after the crash.

d) What is the treatment in emergencies? (2 Marks)

Answer: Immediate administration of IV Dextrose (e.g., 50% Dextrose) if the patient is unconscious, or IM Glucagon if IV access is unavailable.

e) What advice can you give to the patient? (2 Marks)

Answer: 1) Never skip meals, especially when taking insulin. 2) Monitor blood glucose more frequently during illness (sick day rules) and adjust insulin only under medical guidance.

Question 2 [10 Marks Total]

a) What is the diagnosis? (1 Mark)

Answer: Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA).

b) In which condition will the disease in (a) be presented in? (1 Mark)

Answer: Diabetes Mellitus Type 1.

c) What three (3) other conditions can be detected from the biochemical results? (3 Marks)

Answer:

  • Metabolic Acidosis: Indicated by pH 7.10 and low bicarbonate (10 mmol/L).
  • Hyperkalemia: Potassium is 5.9 mmol/L (normal: 3.5-5.0).
  • Hyponatremia: Sodium is slightly low at 130 mmol/L.

d) Explain briefly how the condition mentioned in (b) is brought about (3 Marks)

Answer: Absolute insulin deficiency prevents cells from using glucose for energy. The body compensates by breaking down fats (lipolysis), leading to an overproduction of ketone bodies (acetoacetate, beta-hydroxybutyrate), which are acidic and lower the blood pH.

e) State two principles of managing the condition in (b) (2 Marks)

Answer: 1) Fluid Resuscitation: IV normal saline to correct dehydration. 2) Insulin Therapy: IV insulin to lower glucose and stop ketone production.

Question 3 [10 Marks Total]

a) What is the tentative diagnosis? (2 Marks)

Answer: Gouty Arthritis (Gout).

b) What laboratory tests would you carry out to arrive at the definitive diagnosis? (2 Marks)

Answer: 1) Synovial fluid analysis to identify Monosodium Urate (MSU) crystals. 2) Serum Uric Acid levels.

c) What is the biochemical basis of the condition in (a)? (3 Marks)

Answer: It is caused by Hyperuricemia (excess uric acid in the blood). This results from either overproduction of uric acid due to purine catabolism (often fueled by high-purine diets/alcohol) or under-excretion by the kidneys.

d) List the interventions that you would suggest as a Medical Doctor in charge (2 Marks)

Answer: 1) NSAIDs or Colchicine to reduce acute inflammation/pain. 2) Allopurinol (Xanthine oxidase inhibitor) for long-term management to lower uric acid production.

e) What advice would you give to the patient? (1 Mark)

Answer: Dietary modification: Avoid high-purine foods (red meats, seafood) and limit alcohol consumption, particularly beer.
