Short Essay Questions

1. Describe the following models of enzyme action (4 marks)

Model Answers

i. Induced fit: The shape of the active site of the enzyme is not complementary to that of the substrate. It becomes complementary only when the substrate binds; the shape of the active site is not fixed.

ii. Lock and Key: The shape of the active site of the enzyme is complementary to that of the substrate even before the substrate binds; the shape of the active site is fixed.

2. Explain how a metabolic pathway can be self-regulating, i.e., how it can keep itself off and on (1 mark)

Model Answer

When the end product of the pathway is in excess, it binds to an allosteric site on Enzyme 1 and changes the active site, rendering it incapable of binding the substrate.

3. Explain how denaturation of an enzyme affects its activity. State the effect of the denaturant on each of the structures of the protein enzyme. Do you envisage return of enzyme activity when the denaturant is removed? (2 marks)

Model Answer

Denaturation changes the conformation, causing loss of secondary and tertiary structures. There is no effect on the primary structure. Return of activity is possible (Yes), depending on the extent of denaturation.

4. State what happens to the rate of product formation in an enzyme-catalyzed reaction if you continue to:

Model Answers

i. Increase until enzyme is saturated.

ii. Decrease in product formation.

iii. Increase for as long as the substrate is present.

7. Explain the statement that an enzyme does not change the position of the equilibrium of the reaction (1 mark)

Model Answer

An enzyme does not make an unfavourable reaction favourable.

8. What is the role of Methemoglobin in cyanide poisoning? (2 marks)

Model Answer

Methemoglobin easily binds cyanide (high affinity) to form cyanomethemoglobin, and so this reduces the poisonous effect of cyanide.

9. Explain why increased amounts of glycosylated Hemoglobin is found in patients with Diabetes mellitus (2 marks)

Model Answer

About 5% of HbA is glycated in normal human beings. In diabetes, since the glucose level is high, it therefore follows that even more HbA will be glycated.

10. Explain how, in Hemoglobin, the substitution of the non-polar amino acid for a charged amino acid residue in patients with sickle cell disease results in interruption of oxygen to tissues (2 marks)

Model Answer

The red blood cells tend to sickle (polymerize) under low oxygen tension, becoming rigid and blocking small capillaries, which prevents oxygen delivery.

11. Explain the selective advantage for sickle cell heterozygotes in as far as infection with Plasmodium falciparum is concerned (2 marks)

Model Answer

The parasite does not complete its life cycle inside the red blood cells as the red blood cells have a shorter half-life.
