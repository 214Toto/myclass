Model Answers

a) The two strands run in opposite directions; one strand is oriented in the 5' to 3' direction, while its complementary strand is aligned 3' to 5'.

b) Approximately 3.1 billion base pairs (3.1 X 10^9 base pairs) per haploid genome.

c) Approximately 10 base pairs (often rounded to 10) per full structural helical turn in standard B-DNA.

d)

  1. It must contain complex information in a stable chemical structure.
  2. It must replicate with high precision to preserve characteristics across generations.

e) The Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty Experiment (1944): They demonstrated that DNA (and not protein or RNA) was the "transforming principle" responsible for turning harmless R-strain Streptococcus pneumoniae bacteria into the virulent S-strain.
The Hershey and Chase Experiment (1952): Using radioactive isotopes 32P and 35S to label bacteriophages, they definitively proved that DNA is the specific hereditary material injected into bacterial cells to direct the generation of new viruses.

h) A-DNA: A right-handed helix that is shorter, wider, and more compact than B-DNA. It forms under dehydrated (low water) conditions. Has about 11 Base Pairs per turn
Z-DNA: A left-handed helix with a characteristic zigzag sugar-phosphate backbone. It forms in regions with alternating purine-pyrimidine sequences (e.g., GC repeats) and high salt concentrations. Has about 12 base pairs per turn.

i) The Meselson-Stahl experiment proved that DNA replication is semiconservative (where each strand of the original molecule serves as a template for a new strand).
The Mechanism of Replication - Unwind: Helicase opens the DNA helix at the origin (ori) to create a replication fork. Topoisomerase (DNA gyrase) relieves mechanical strain ahead of it, and SSB proteins keep the strands separated.
Build: Primase lays down a short RNA primer. DNA Polymerase III builds new strands only in the 5' to 3' direction: Leading strand: Built continuously toward the fork. Lagging strand: Built discontinuously away from the fork in short Okazaki fragments.
Seal: DNA Polymerase I replaces the RNA primers with DNA, and DNA Ligase glues the fragments together.

As drugs, they stall transcription/replication or create massive replication errors, selectively halting rapidly dividing cancerous or viral cell populations.

l) 3'-TGCGTAT-5' (or 5'-TATGCGT-3')

n) (a) Secondary structure of tRNA (Cloverleaf model)
(b) Tertiary structure of tRNA (L-shaped model)

o) It protects the mature transcript from enzymatic degradation by 3' exonucleases, assists in nuclear export pathways, and dramatically increases translation efficiency on cytoplasmic ribosomes.

p) It acts as the ribosome-binding site that pairs with the complementary 16S rRNA sequence within the small (30S) bacterial ribosomal subunit, precisely positioning the start codon (AUG) to initiate protein synthesis.

q) It is formed when a single strand of RNA contains local inverted palindrome sequences. The single strand loops back on itself, forming complementary hydrogen-bonded base pairs (the stem) while leaving un-paired nucleotides at the bend (the loop).

r) Phosphodiester bonds (linking the 3'-hydroxyl of one ribose to the 5'-phosphate of the next).

s) Ribosomal RNA (rRNA), Transfer RNA (tRNA), and Messenger RNA (mRNA). Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) accounts for the highest percentage (approx 80%) of total cellular RNA.

t) RNA is typically a single-stranded molecule. Because it does not exist as a double-stranded helix along its entire length, it does not conform to Chargaff's rules ($\text{A}+\text{G} \neq \text{C}+\text{U}$).

u) RNA contains a 2'-hydroxyl (-OH) group on its ribose sugar component. This group behaves as a nucleophile under basic conditions, attacking the adjacent phosphodiester bond to break the backbone. Deoxyribose in DNA lacks this 2'-OH group, making it highly stable against alkaline hydrolysis.
