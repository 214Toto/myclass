Short Essay Questions

1. Diarrhea from infection or malnutrition is the world’s most prevalent killer of children. A 2-month-old Caucasian girl develops chronic diarrhea and liver inflammation in early infancy when the mother begins using formula that includes corn syrup. Evaluation of the child demonstrates sensitivity to fructose in the diet.

Model Answers

a) Hereditary Fructose Intolerance (HFI).

b) Sucrose.

c) Sucrose consists of α-D-glucose linked to β-D-fructose through an α(1→2)β glycosidic bond.

d) α(1→2)β glycosidic bond.

e) Sucrose is hydrolyzed by the enzyme sucrase (invertase) in the small intestine into glucose and fructose. Glucose is absorbed via SGLT-1 while fructose is absorbed via GLUT-5; both exit enterocytes via GLUT-2.

f) Fructose contains a ketone (keto) functional group. The qualitative test used to identify ketoses is Seliwanoff’s test.

g) Fructose is a reducing sugar because it can tautomerize under alkaline conditions to form aldoses that reduce copper ions in Benedict’s or Fehling’s reagent.

h) Benedict’s test. A color change from blue → green → yellow → orange → brick-red indicates increasing concentrations of reducing sugars.

i) Fructosazone. It forms needle-shaped crystals similar to glucosazone; fructose and glucose produce the same osazone crystals because they differ only at C-1 and C-2.

j) To provide an energy source for spermatozoa in semen, supporting sperm motility and viability.

k) Fructose is a ketohexose sugar, whereas fucose is a 6-deoxyhexose sugar. Fucose is important as a component of blood group antigens and glycoproteins.

l) Inulin. Clinically important because it is used to measure glomerular filtration rate (GFR) as it is freely filtered and neither reabsorbed nor secreted by the kidneys.

2. A 14-year-old African American adolescent, who is fed on milk-based foods regularly, presents with abdominal discomfort, abdominal fullness, excess gas, and weight loss. Blood glucose, cholesterol and alkaline phosphatase levels are normal. There is no jaundice or elevations of liver transaminases. The stool tests positive for reducing substances.

Model Answers

a) Lactose intolerance.

b) Lactase (β-galactosidase) deficiency.

c) Undigested lactose remains in the intestinal lumen causing osmotic diarrhea. Colonic bacteria ferment lactose producing gases (H₂, CO₂, CH₄) and organic acids, resulting in abdominal discomfort, bloating and flatulence.

d) Lactose.

e) Glucose and galactose.

f) Galactose → Keratan sulfate (important in cornea and cartilage). Glucose → Hyaluronic acid (important for lubrication of joints and extracellular matrix).

g) Lactosazone; forms powder-puff (cotton-ball/hedgehog) shaped crystals.

h) Lactose is less cariogenic than glucose and fructose because it is fermented less readily by oral bacteria.

3. A 45-year-old Caucasian male is found to have a serum cholesterol of 300 mg/dL after a 12-hour fast. The physician cautioned the patient about the possibilities of coronary heart disease (heart attack) and stroke, and put him on statins to keep serum cholesterol below recommended limits and maximize levels of high-density lipoprotein (HDL).

Model Answers

a) A lipoprotein is a complex of lipids and proteins (apolipoproteins) that transports lipids in blood.

b) Lipoproteins are non-covalent complexes that transport lipids in plasma, whereas proteolipids are proteins tightly associated with lipids, commonly found in biological membranes.

c) Transport of lipids (cholesterol, triglycerides and phospholipids) in blood.

d) HDL is the “good” lipoprotein because it removes excess cholesterol from tissues and returns it to the liver (reverse cholesterol transport).

e) LDL is the “bad” lipoprotein because it delivers cholesterol to peripheral tissues and promotes atherosclerotic plaque formation.

f) LDL (Low-Density Lipoprotein).

g) HDL (High-Density Lipoprotein).

h) Desirable total serum cholesterol: less than 200 mg/dL.

i) LDL and HDL.

j) LDL.

k) Elevated LDL cholesterol promotes atherosclerotic plaque formation in coronary arteries, narrowing the lumen and reducing blood flow to the heart, which may lead to myocardial infarction.

l) Atherosclerotic plaques can develop in cerebral or carotid arteries causing vessel narrowing or thrombosis, reducing blood supply to the brain and resulting in stroke.

4. Name the enzyme deficiencies and the accumulating metabolites in the following lipid storage diseases.

Model Answers

b) Enzyme Deficiency: $\beta$-Hexosaminidase A and B
Accumulating Metabolite: $\mathrm{GM_2}$ ganglioside and globosides

c) Enzyme Deficiency: $\alpha$-Galactosidase A
Accumulating Metabolite: Globotriaosylceramide ($\mathrm{Gb_3}$ / Ceramide trihexoside)

e) Enzyme Deficiency: Lysosomal Acid Lipase (LAL)
Accumulating Metabolite: Cholesteryl esters and triglycerides

5. The nucleic acids from three viruses were extracted and examined to determine their base composition, and given the following results.
(i) 35% A, 35% T, 15% G, and 15% C;
(ii) 35% A, 15% T, 25% G, and 25% C.
(iii) 35% A, 30% U, 30% G, and 5% C.

Model Answers

a) It is a Double-Stranded DNA (dsDNA) genome. The presence of Thymine (T) confirms it is DNA, and the equal proportions of adenine to thymine ($\text{A}=\text{T}=35\%$) and guanine to cytosine ($\text{G}=\text{C}=15\%$) conform to Chargaff's rules of base pairing.

b) It is a Single-Stranded DNA (ssDNA) genome. The presence of Thymine (T) points to DNA, but the unequal proportions of complementary bases ($\text{A} \neq \text{T}$ and $\text{G} = \text{C}$ by coincidence) violate Chargaff's rules for double-stranded structures.

c) It is a Single-Stranded RNA (ssRNA) genome. The presence of Uracil (U) instead of Thymine (T) establishes that it is RNA, and the asymmetric base ratios ($\text{A} \neq \text{U}$ and $\text{G} \neq \text{C}$) reveal it is single-stranded.

d) A genome is the complete set of genetic material or hereditary information contained within an organism or a virus, which can consist of either DNA or RNA.

e) A genome encompasses the entire collective genetic composition of an organism, whereas a gene is a distinct, localized sub-unit of inheritance within that genome that codes for a specific protein or functional RNA molecule.

f) A nucleotide is formed by the combination of a pentose sugar (ribose or deoxyribose), a phosphate group, and a nitrogenous base. The components are linked together by N-glycosidic bonds (connecting the base to the sugar) and phosphoester bonds (connecting the phosphate to the sugar), while nucleotides are connected via phosphodiester bonds along the backbone and stabilized by hydrogen bonds between complementary strands.

g)

  • Chemically: DNA contains deoxyribose sugar and the base thymine (T), whereas RNA contains ribose sugar and the base uracil (U).
  • Functionally: DNA long-term stores and preserves primary genetic blueprints, while RNA transiently carries, transfers, and processes that instruction set to synthesize proteins.
  • Location: DNA is primarily restricted to the nucleus (and mitochondria/chloroplasts), while RNA is synthesized in the nucleus but predominantly functions in the cytoplasm and ribosomes.

h) 3'-TAACGGCAG-5' (or 5'-GACGGCAAT-3')

i) 3'-UAACGGCAG-5' (or 5'-GACGGCAAU-3')

j) $141 \text{ codons} \times 3 \text{ nucleotides/codon} = 423\text{ nucleotides}$.

k) Guanine-Cytosine ($\text{G}\equiv\text{C}$) base pairs are held together by three hydrogen bonds, whereas Adenine-Thymine ($\text{A}=\text{T}$) pairs share only two. Consequently, higher GC content delivers enhanced thermodynamic stability to the double helix, demanding greater thermal energy to disrupt the strands.

6. DNA replication is carried out in all organisms by a multiprotein complex called the replisome. DNA synthesis always takes place from the 5' to the 3' end. The template strands have opposite directions. Eukaryotic DNA replication is more complex than prokaryotic replication.

Model Answers

a) High fidelity prevents permanent, deleterious mutations and DNA damage that could alter critical proteins, disrupt cell homeostasis, or lead to non-viable offspring or malignancies.

b) Fidelity is maintained by the strict structural constraints of active-site base-pairing geometry in DNA polymerases, its intrinsic proofreading exonuclease activity, post-replication mismatch repair (MMR) systems, and cellular cell cycle control pathways (such as the p53 tumor suppressor gene protein cascades).

c) The 3' to 5' exonuclease activity of the replicating DNA polymerase.

d) Eukaryotes manage complex linear chromosomes across multiple replication origins, requiring specialized polymerases to handle initiation, distinct leading/lagging strand synthesis, translesion repair synthesis over damaged regions, telomere maintenance, and organelle (mitochondrial) DNA synthesis.

e) Lagging strand

f) Eukaryotic DNA is wound tightly around histone octamers forming nucleosomes within complex chromatin architectures that must be disassembled and precisely reassembled during replication, alongside organizing initiation across thousands of firing replication origins simultaneously compared to a bacterium's single origin.

g)

  • DNA Gyrase Inhibitor: Ciprofloxacin (or Novobiocin / Nalidixic acid)
  • DNA Polymerase Inhibitor: Aphidicolin (or Ara-C / Cytarabine for eukaryotic counterparts; Phosphonoformate for viral variants)

7. A 10-year-old girl is brought to the dermatologist by her parents. She has many freckles on her face, neck, arms, and hands, and the parents report that she is unusually sensitive to sunlight. Two basal cell carcinomas are identified on her face.
Diagnosis: Xeroderma pigmentosum

Model Answers

a) Nucleotide Excision Repair (NER)

b) An inherited genetic mutation resulting in a deficiency or complete loss of functioning NER proteins / XP enzymes (such as UV-specific endonucleases, helicases, or photolyase variants in lower systems).

c) Pyrimidine dimers (specifically, cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers [CPDs] or 6-4 photoproducts) induced by ultraviolet (UV) radiation.

d)

  1. Deamination of bases (e.g., cytosine to uracil) → Repaired via Base Excision Repair (BER)
  2. Double-strand breaks (DSBs) → Repaired via Non-Homologous End Joining (NHEJ) or Homologous Recombination (HR)

e) Mismatch Repair (MMR)

f) DNA serves as the permanent, irreplaceable blueprint of life; a modification permanently alters the cell lineage. Conversely, mRNA and proteins are transient, continuously degraded, and can simply be re-transcribed or re-translated from an uncompromised master DNA copy without lingering systemic injury.
