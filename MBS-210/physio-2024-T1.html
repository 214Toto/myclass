Short Essay Questions

26. Michael Mumba, LeBron James, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar are men who have changed the history of basketball. What would happen to membrane electricity, if one of these players had increased potassium in the ECF. Outline the physiology that would be disrupted at plasma membrane level. [5 marks]

Model Answer

Increased extracellular potassium (hyperkalemia) decreases the concentration gradient for K+ exit from the cell. According to the Nernst equation, this causes depolarization of the Resting Membrane Potential (making it less negative). Initially, this brings the membrane closer to threshold, increasing excitability; however, persistent depolarization leads to the inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels, ultimately decreasing excitability and disrupting action potential conduction in excitable tissues like the heart and skeletal muscle.

27. What would happen if one of these players had electrolyte derangements during the game? Outline the physiological balance and distribution of normal electrolytes in body fluids. [5 marks]

Model Answer

Electrolytes are asymmetrically distributed: Extracellular Fluid (ECF) is rich in Sodium ($Na^+$) and Chloride ($Cl^-$), while Intracellular Fluid (ICF) is rich in Potassium ($K^+$) and Phosphates. Derangements (e.g., hyponatremia or hypokalemia) disrupt osmotic balance, leading to cellular swelling or shrinkage, and impair neuromuscular transmission and cardiac rhythm, potentially causing muscle cramps, arrhythmias, or neurological deficits during physical exertion.

28. Compare and contrast the applied CLINICAL significance of osmolarity and osmolality. [5 marks]

Model Answer

Osmolarity is the concentration of solutes per liter of solution (volumetric), which can be affected by temperature and pressure. Osmolality is the concentration per kilogram of solvent (mass) and is more clinically accurate because it is independent of temperature. Osmolality is more accurate than osmolarity, because volume varies with temperature, but weight does not.

29. Discuss succinctly the types of IV fluids that are needed to correct his electrolyte imbalance based on tonicity. [5 marks]

Model Answer

Isotonic fluids (e.g., 0.9% NaCl) have the same effective osmolality as plasma; they expand ECF volume without causing fluid shifts. Hypotonic fluids (e.g., 0.45% NaCl) have lower tonicity, causing water to move from ECF into cells (ICF), used for cellular dehydration. Hypertonic fluids (e.g., 3% NaCl) have higher tonicity, drawing water out of cells into the ECF, used cautiously to treat severe symptomatic hyponatremia or cerebral edema.

30. COMPARE and CONTRAST the physiology of gap junctions and tight junctions with needed EXAMPLES. [5 marks]

Model Answer

Tight Junctions (Zonula Occludens) create a "leak-proof" seal between cells to prevent the movement of molecules between them; Example: Blood-brain barrier or intestinal epithelium. Gap Junctions consist of connexons that form channels allowing direct electrical and chemical communication between adjacent cells; Example: Cardiac muscle cells (allowing synchronized contraction) or smooth muscle in the GI tract.

3. As per UN reports, nearly 40% of people in Tigray region of Ethiopia are currently suffering from lack of food to eat.

Model Answers

a) Malnutrition leads to mucosal atrophy and loss of digestive enzymes. Unabsorbed solutes remain in the intestinal lumen, creating an osmotic gradient that draws water out of the ECF into the gut (osmotic diarrhea), leading to severe dehydration and hypovolemia.

b) Serum Electrolyte Panel: Sodium (135–145 mEq/L), Potassium (3.5–5.0 mEq/L), Chloride (98–107 mEq/L), and Bicarbonate (22–28 mEq/L). Also, Urinalysis and Serum Albumin to assess protein status.

c) The plasma membrane acts as a semi-permeable barrier. Ion channels allow facilitated diffusion of specific ions, while Active Transport (like the $Na^+/K^+$ ATPase pump) moves ions against their gradients using ATP to maintain high intracellular $K^+$ and high extracellular $Na^+$.

d) The Donnan effect (accumulation of ions due to non-diffusible intracellular proteins) is potentially hazardous because it would cause water to enter cells by osmosis, leading to cell bursting. It is prevented by the $Na^+/K^+$ ATPase pump, which actively extrudes 3 $Na^+$ ions for every 2 $K^+$ ions brought in, effectively "pumping out" the excess osmotic pressure.
