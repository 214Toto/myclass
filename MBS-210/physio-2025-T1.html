Short Essay Questions

1. Explain the operational effect of anaesthetics on nerve physiology, with examples.

Model Answers

Anaesthetics primarily act by blocking voltage-gated sodium channels in the nerve membrane, preventing depolarization and the propagation of action potentials. Examples: Lidocaine (local) and Propofol or Isoflurane (general).

2. Describe the functions of integrins in the cell and tight junctions between cells.

Model Answers

Integrins: Mediate cell-extracellular matrix (ECM) adhesion and signal transduction. Tight Junctions: Create a seal between adjacent epithelial cells to prevent the leakage of molecules (paracellular barrier) and maintain cell polarity.

3. Elucidate the structural design, with a labelled diagram, of nexus in body cells and write a note on their physiological significance.

Model Answers

Nexus (Gap Junctions): Composed of transmembrane proteins called connexins which form hexameric structures known as connexons. Physiological Significance: They allow direct electrical and chemical communication between cells, crucial for synchronized contraction in cardiac and smooth muscle.

4. In the membrane of cardiac muscle, intracellular Ca²⁺ is exchanged for extracellular Na⁺. Explain this type of transport and give three (3) other relevant examples.

Model Answers

This is Secondary Active Transport (Antiport/Counter-transport), specifically the Sodium-Calcium Exchanger (NCX). Examples: 1. Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger (pH regulation), 2. Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger (Chloride shift), 3. Na⁺/Glucose symport (SGLT - though a symport, it is the same category of transport).

5. Explain phagocytosis with a labelled diagram.

Model Answers

Phagocytosis is the process by which a cell (like a macrophage) engulfs large particles or pathogens. Stages include chemotaxis, adherence, ingestion (forming a phagosome), fusion with lysosomes (forming a phagolysosome), and digestion.

6. Draw a labelled concept diagram of the Donnan effect and explain its significance.

Model Answers

The Donnan Effect describes the behavior of charged particles near a semi-permeable membrane that fail to distribute evenly due to the presence of non-diffusible ions (like proteins). Significance: It contributes to the resting membrane potential and influences osmotic pressure within cells and capillaries.

7. Elucidate the concepts of IPSP, EPSP, and depolarization in a nerve membrane.

Model Answers

EPSP: Excitatory Postsynaptic Potential (graded depolarization). IPSP: Inhibitory Postsynaptic Potential (graded hyperpolarization). Depolarization: A change in membrane potential where the interior becomes less negative, moving toward the threshold for an action potential.

8. Describe the pathophysiology of diarrhea in cholera disease, with examples of IV fluids used at Ndola Hitech Hospital.

Model Answers

Cholera toxin increases intracellular cAMP, leading to massive secretion of Cl⁻ and water into the intestinal lumen, causing "rice-water" stools. IV Fluids: Ringer's Lactate (preferred for electrolyte replacement) and Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl).

1. A medical science student, Ms. Aniya, who has been experiencing persistent insomnia for several weeks. Her family doctor prescribes a sleeping pill known to enhance the effects of GABA in the brain.

Model Answers

GABA Mechanism: GABA is an inhibitory neurotransmitter. Enhancing its effect increases Cl⁻ influx into the post-synaptic neuron, causing hyperpolarization. This moves the membrane potential further from the threshold, making action potentials less likely, thus reducing neuronal excitability and promoting sleep.

Myasthenia Gravis: An autoimmune disorder where antibodies block or destroy nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the NMJ. This results in reduced end-plate potentials, failure of muscle fiber action potentials, and progressive muscle weakness.

2. A researcher is investigating the neural basis of different behavioral states in human subjects. They administer various pharmacological agents and observe the resulting changes in physiological responses and reported experiences.

Model Answers

  1. Acetylcholine: Triggers muscle contraction at the NMJ; parasympathetic effects.
  2. Dopamine: Involved in reward, motivation, and motor control (Basal ganglia).
  3. Serotoning: Regulates mood, sleep, and appetite.
  4. Glutamate: The primary excitatory neurotransmitter in the CNS.
  5. Norepinephrine: Mediates 'fight or flight' responses (Sympathetic).

3. A neuroscientist is studying the activity of a neuron in the brain of a research participant...

Model Answers

Spatial Summation: Multiple presynaptic neurons fire simultaneously at different locations on the post-synaptic neuron to reach threshold.

Temporal Summation: A single presynaptic neuron fires in rapid succession, allowing potentials to build up over time to reach threshold.

Smooth Muscle AP: Can occur as "spike potentials" or action potentials with a plateau. Some exhibit "slow wave" rhythmicity which can trigger APs without external stimuli.

4. An old man came to NTH with chest pain for a week.

Model Answers

Phases: 0 (Depolarization-Na⁺), 1 (Brief repolarization), 2 (Plateau-Ca²⁺ influx), 3 (Repolarization-K⁺ efflux), 4 (Resting). The long plateau phase results in a long Absolute Refractory Period (ARP), ensuring the muscle relaxes before it can be stimulated again, thus preventing tetany.

5. The human heart has four chambers, unlike an octopus, which has two hearts in its body.

Model Answers

Automaticity is due to a slow, spontaneous depolarization (prepotential). It is caused by the opening of "funny" Na⁺ channels (If), followed by transient T-type Ca²⁺ channels, which bring the membrane to threshold without neural input.

Section E: 1. Draw a labelled diagram of the pump that resolves the problem caused by the Donnan Equilibrium in the cells.

Model Answers

The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump (Sodium-Potassium pump). It actively pumps 3 Na⁺ ions out and 2 K⁺ ions in. This prevents the osmotic swelling of the cell that would otherwise be caused by the Donnan Effect.
