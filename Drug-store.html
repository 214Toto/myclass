Drug Library

Thiazides

Diuretic
Mechanism of Action
  • Reduces BP (blood pressure) by reducing sodium stores, blood volume, and CO (cardiac output).
  • Sodium is believed to contribute to vascular resistance by increasing vessel stiffness and neural reactivity.
Indications
  • Monotherapy in mild-moderate hypertension
  • Combined with sympatholytics or vasodilators in severe hypertension (to counteract salt and water retention induced by those agents)
  • Recommended in elderly patients
Side Effects
  • Potassium depletion / Hypokalemia
  • Magnesium depletion
  • Impaired glucose tolerance
  • Increased serum lipid concentrations

Frusemide

Loop Diuretic
Mechanism of Action
  • Reduces BP (blood pressure) by reducing sodium stores, blood volume, and CO (cardiac output) via action on the Loop of Henle.
Indications
  • Severe hypertension
  • Hypertension with co-existing chronic renal failure
  • Hypertension with co-existing CCF (Congestive Cardiac Failure)
  • Resistance to combination therapy
Side Effects
  • Potassium depletion / Hypokalemia
  • Magnesium depletion
  • Impaired glucose tolerance
  • Increased serum lipid concentrations

Labetalol

Alpha/Beta Blocker
Mechanism of Action
  • Blocks both alpha and beta-adrenergic receptors to rapidly reduce systemic vascular resistance and blood pressure.
Indications
  • Hypertensive emergencies (administered intravenously)
Side Effects & Features
  • Does not produce salt and water retention

Carvedilol

Beta Blocker with α1-Blocking Activity
Mechanism of Action
  • Combines non-selective beta-blockade with additional α1-receptor blocking effects, leading to peripheral vasodilation.
Indications
  • Hypertension
  • Useful when both heart rate reduction and direct vasodilation are therapeutic goals
Side Effects & Features
  • Does not produce salt and water retention

Atenolol

Selective Beta Blocker (β1)
Mechanism of Action
  • Selectively blocks β1 receptors in the heart and juxtaglomerular cells.
  • Lowers CO or PVR (peripheral vascular resistance) to various degrees depending on cardioselectivity.
Indications
  • Hypertension (Effective for all grades)
  • Recommended in young, non-obese patients
  • Used in combination with vasodilators in severe hypertension to prevent reflex tachycardia
Side Effects & Features
  • Does not produce postural hypotension
  • Does not produce salt and water retention

Propranolol

Non-Selective Beta Blocker
Mechanism of Action
  • Blocks beta receptors in the heart and juxtaglomerular cells.
  • Decreases blood pressure primarily by lowering cardiac output (CO).
  • Inhibits the stimulation of renin production by catecholamines (mediated by β1 receptors).
Indications
  • Hypertension (Effective for all grades)
  • Recommended in young, non-obese patients
  • Used in combination with vasodilators in severe hypertension to prevent reflex tachycardia
Side Effects & Features
  • Does not produce postural hypotension
  • Does not produce salt and water retention

ACE Inhibitors

Angiotensin Converting Enzyme Inhibitors
Mechanism of Action & Physiological Effects
  • Vascular Effects: Reduces PVR (Peripheral Vascular Resistance) via arteriolar and venular dilatation without causing postural hypotension. Promotes natriuresis.
  • Cardiac Effects: Induces minimal change in CO or heart rate (blunts sympathetic reflexes and inhibits Angiotensin II-induced norepinephrine release, preventing reflex tachycardia). Decreases preload/filling pressures, improving pulmonary and venous congestion.
  • Renal & Cerebral Protection: Reduces renovascular resistance to maintain renal blood flow. Cerebral and coronary blood flows are well maintained.
  • Ventricular Remodeling: Reverses ventricular remodeling by reducing preload/afterload, preventing the trophic effects of Angiotensin II on myocytes, and attenuating aldosterone-induced cardiac fibrosis.
Indications & Therapeutic Uses
  • Essential Hypertension: First-line drug as monotherapy in mild-to-moderate cases, or combined with diuretics, CCBs, or β-blockers.
  • Hypertension with Co-morbidities: 1st choice for patients with diabetes or nephropathy (improves glucose tolerance). Most appropriate for left ventricular hypertrophy and high-renin hypertension (e.g., unilateral renal artery stenosis).
  • Congestive Cardiac Failure (CCF): Combined with a diuretic to delay the progress of heart failure and improve quality of life.
  • Post-Myocardial Infarction: Therapy started immediately after infarction to prevent myocardial hypertrophy, preserve left ventricular function, and aid remodeling.
Side Effects & Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)
  • First-dose hypotension (mitigated by starting with a low dose)
  • Persistent dry cough (due to preservation of kinins causing vasodilation)
  • Skin rash
  • Temporary loss of taste
  • Proteinuria and nephrotic syndrome (particularly in patients with pre-existing renal disease)
  • Neutropenia (Rare but serious)
  • No metabolic adverse effects (does not alter blood uric acid or cholesterol levels)
Drug Interactions
  • $K^+$ supplements and $K^+$-sparing diuretics: Risk of inducing hyperkalemia due to the reduction of aldosterone secretion.
  • NSAIDs: Attenuates the antihypertensive effects of ACEIs.

ARBs

Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers
Mechanism of Action
  • Provides a more complete inhibition of the effect of Angiotensin II compared to ACE inhibitors.
  • Has no effect on bradykinin metabolism (meaning it does not cause the dry cough associated with kinin accumulation).
Indications
  • Hypertension
  • Often used as an alternative for patients who cannot tolerate ACE inhibitors due to dry cough.
Side Effects & Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)
  • Foetopathic (contraindicated in pregnancy)
  • Severe hypotension in hypovolemic patients
  • Hyperkalaemia in patients with underlying renal disease

Dihydropyridines

Calcium Channel Blocker (Vascular Selective)
Mechanism & Physiological Effects
  • Vascular Smooth Muscle: Highly potent at relaxing arterial smooth muscles (Dihydropyridines > Verapamil > Diltiazem). They relax vascular smooth muscles at much lower concentrations than those required to induce negative inotropism.
  • Cardiac Effects: Vasodilation leads to reflex sympathetic activation, which clinically overcomes their intrinsic negative inotropic effect.
  • AV Nodal Conduction: Nifedipine has no effect on AV conduction.
  • Hemodynamics: Decreases blood pressure (BP) and decreases the overall work done by the heart via reduced contractility and vasodilation.
Indications
  • Preferable in elderly hypertensives.
  • Effective in low renin hypertension (provided there is no Heart Failure [HF] and no volume depletion).

Verapamil & Diltiazem

Calcium Channel Blocker (Cardioselective)
Mechanism & Physiological Effects
  • Myocardial Contractility: All CCBs produce a negative inotropic effect, but it is most pronounced in this class (Verapamil > Diltiazem).
  • AV Nodal Conduction: Verapamil and Diltiazem significantly decrease heart rate (HR) and delay AV conduction.
  • Vascular Effect: Relax arterial smooth muscles, but less potently than dihydropyridines.
  • Hemodynamics: Decreases blood pressure (BP) and decreases the work done by the heart by directly lowering contractility and heart rate.
Indications
  • Preferable in elderly hypertensives.
  • Effective in low renin hypertension (provided there is no Heart Failure [HF] and no volume depletion).
  • Particularly useful when hypertension co-exists with a need for heart rate control (e.g., certain tachyarrhythmias).

Hydralazine

Vasodilator
Mechanism of Action
  • Molecular mechanism is not fully known.
Indications
  • Used in severe hypertension.
  • Cannot be used as monotherapy due to the rapid development of tachyphylaxis.
Side Effects & Management
  • Increases Heart Rate (HR) and Cardiac Output (CO) via reflex sympathetic activation; must be combined with β-blockers.
  • Causes fluid retention; must be combined with a diuretic.

Minoxidil

Arteriolar Dilator
Mechanism of Action
  • Opens K+ channels in vascular smooth muscle membranes, hyperpolarizing and relaxing the tissue.
Indications
  • Alternative to hydralazine when maximal doses of hydralazine are not effective.
  • Used in combination with diuretics and β-blockers to avoid fluid retention and tachyphylaxis.
  • Topical minoxidil is used as a hair growth stimulant in alopecia.

Sodium Nitroprusside

Parenteral Vasodilator
Mechanism & Handling
  • Rapidly metabolized by uptake into RBCs with the liberation of cyanide.
  • Highly sensitive to light: solution must be freshly prepared and protected by covering with opaque foil.
Toxicity & Adverse Effects
  • Cyanide Accumulation: Can lead to metabolic acidosis, excessive hypotension, arrhythmias, and methaemoglobinemia.
Antidote / Treatment
  • Treatment of cyanide toxicity includes administration of hydroxycobalamine and sodium thiosulfate.

Diazoxide

Vasodilator
Mechanism of Action
  • Opens K+ channels and stabilizes the vascular smooth muscle membrane potential.
  • Chemically similar to thiazide diuretics but possesses absolutely no diuretic effect.
Administration & Dosage
  • Given by rapid IV infusion at a rate of 15 mg/min until blood pressure (BP) is controlled.

Fenoldopam

D1 Agonist / Arteriolar Dilator
Mechanism of Action
  • Acts as a selective dopamine D1 receptor agonist.
Indications
  • Functions as an arteriolar dilator utilized strictly for hypertensive emergencies.

Quinidine

Class Ia Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Na+ channel blocker: Reduces the maximum rate of depolarization (Vmax), decreasing conduction velocity and depressing excitability.
  • Inhibits spontaneous depolarization of automatic fibers, decreasing their rate of discharge.
  • Increases action potential duration (APD) through prolonging repolarization by blocking K+ channels.
  • Exhibits autonomic effects: Atropine-like action and Alpha-blocking action (causing hypotension and reflex tachycardia).
Indications
  • Broad spectrum anti-arrhythmic, especially used in Atrial flutter or Atrial fibrillation refractory to other agents.
Side Effects
  • Cardiac toxicity: SAN block, AVN block, ventricular arrhythmias, syncope, death, and paradoxical ventricular tachycardia in patients with Atrial fibrillation.
  • Extracardiac toxicity: GIT upset, Hypotension.
  • Cinchonism: Tinnitus, loss of hearing, blurred vision, and photophobia.
  • Other: Drug fever, thrombocytopenia, anaphylactic shock, and embolism following conversion of Atrial fibrillation to sinus rhythm.

Disopyramide

Class Ia Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Na+ channel blocker that decreases conduction velocity and responsiveness.
  • Similar to Quinidine with a marked anti-cholinergic effect.
  • Exhibits a marked negative inotropic effect.
Indications
  • Broad spectrum anti-arrhythmic used in:
  • Ventricular arrhythmias.
  • Supraventricular arrhythmias.
  • Wolff-Parkinson-White Syndrome.
Side Effects
  • Anticholinergic effects (e.g., dry mouth, urinary retention, blurred vision).
  • Negative inotropic effects (can exacerbate or precipitate heart failure).

Procainamide

Class Ia Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Blocks Na+ channels reducing Vmax leading to moderate decrease in conduction velocity.
  • Increases APD through prolonging repolarization (blocks K+ channels).
  • Inhibits spontaneous depolarization of automatic fibers decreasing their rate of discharge.
Indications
  • Broad spectrum Anti-arrhythmic: used in treatment of ventricular arrhythmias after cardiac infarction.
Side Effects
  • Systemic Lupus Erythematosus.
  • Congestive heart failure (negative inotropic effect).

Lidocaine

Class Ib Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Na+ channel blocker that decreases conduction velocity and excitability.
  • Decreases APD through enhancing repolarization (activate K+ channels).
  • No effect on atrial or AVN conduction therefore ineffective in supraventricular arrhythmias.
  • Selective on ventricular tissue especially when ischemic.
Indications
  • Ventricular arrhythmias (in cardiac surgery, acute myocardial infarction, Digitalis toxicity).
Side Effects
  • CNS: Parasthesia, tremors, dizziness, slurred speech and disturbs hearing.
  • Cardiac effects: It is the least cardiotoxic anti-arrhythmic drug. However, in diseased hearts, it may precipitate SAN standstill or worsen impaired conduction.
  • Pharmacokinetics note: Not effective orally due to extensive first pass metabolism. Administered as a loading dose (1 – 2mg/kg) I.V bolus followed by maintenance infusion.

Mexiletine

Class Ib Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Na+ channel blocker (Class Ib characteristics similar to Lidocaine).
Indications
  • Given orally in Ventricular arrhythmias of acute myocardial infarction.
Side Effects
  • Gastrointestinal upset, dizziness, and tremors (typical of Class Ib oral analogues).

Phenytoin

Class Ib Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Na+ channel blocker that alters conduction properties, particularly useful in reversing digitalis-induced conduction alterations.
Indications
  • May be used in Digitalis-induced arrhythmias.
Side Effects
  • Nystagmus, ataxia, drowsiness, and gingival hyperplasia (with prolonged use).

Propafenone

Class Ic Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Na+ channel blocker that produces a marked reduction in conduction velocity.
  • No effect on action potential duration (APD).
  • Possesses complex multi-channel blocking properties: weak Beta-blocking activity, weak K+ channel blocking activity, and weak Ca++ channel blocking properties.
Indications
  • Supraventricular arrhythmias.
  • Ventricular arrhythmias if there is no heart failure.
Side Effects
  • Cardiac arrhythmias.
  • AV block.
  • Heart failure (due to its negative inotropic and weak beta-blocking potential).

Sotalol

Class II Beta-Blocker
Mechanism of Action
  • Blocks intrinsic sympathetic activity resulting in decreased spontaneous depolarization of autonomic fibers.
  • Protects against sympathetic-induced ectopic focus.
  • Possesses K+ channel blocking action and hence prolongs the action potential duration and ERP (Effective Refractory Period).
Indications
  • Supraventricular arrhythmias, particularly those associated with sympathetic overactivity (e.g., Thyrotoxicosis, Exercise-induced arrhythmias, Pheochromocytoma).
  • Prophylactic use to prevent ventricular arrhythmias following myocardial infarction.
Side Effects
  • Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm, fatigue, and potential for Torsades de Pointes (due to prolonged QT interval from K+ channel blockade).

Esmolol

Class II Beta-Blocker
Mechanism of Action
  • Ultra-short-acting beta-blocker that blocks intrinsic sympathetic activity, resulting in decreased spontaneous depolarization of autonomic fibers.
  • Protects against sympathetic-induced ectopic focus.
Indications
  • Used I.V in acute arrhythmias during surgery or emergency.
  • Supraventricular arrhythmias associated with sympathetic overactivity.
Side Effects
  • Infusion-site reactions, hypotension, bradycardia, and heart failure exacerbation.

Amiodarone

Class III Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Heavily iodinated K+ channel blocking agent; blockade of K+ channels delays depolarization, increasing APD and ERP.
  • Possesses multi-channel blocking action: Na+ channel blocking action, weak Ca++ channel blocker, and Beta-receptor blocker.
  • Causes slowing of sinus rate and AVN conduction.
  • Exhibits a vasodilator effect on coronary and peripheral vessels.
Indications
  • Treatment and Prophylaxis of supraventricular arrhythmias.
  • Arrhythmias associated with Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome.
  • Refractory cases of Ventricular arrhythmias.
Side Effects
  • Corneal deposits (reversible).
  • Alteration of thyroid function (due to being heavily iodinated).
  • Photosensitivity.
  • Pulmonary infiltrates and pulmonary fibrosis.
  • Proximal myopathy and neuropathy.
  • AV block and sinus bradycardia.
  • Pharmacokinetics & Interactions note: Long $t_{1/2}$ (weeks), given once daily. Loading dose: 0.8 – 1.2 gram/day for 2-4 weeks; maintenance: 0.2 gram/day. Potentiates Warfarin and increases Digoxin and Quinidine levels.

Sotalol

Class II and III Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Blocks intrinsic sympathetic activity resulting in decreased spontaneous depolarization of autonomic fibers (Class II action).
  • Protects against sympathetic-induced ectopic focus.
  • Possesses K+ channel blocking action and hence prolongs the action potential duration and ERP (Class III action).
Indications
  • Broad spectrum in supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmias.
  • Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome.
  • Particularly useful for arrhythmias associated with sympathetic overactivity (e.g., Thyrotoxicosis, Exercise-induced arrhythmias, Pheochromocytoma).
  • Prophylactic use to prevent ventricular arrhythmias following myocardial infarction.
Side Effects
  • Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm, fatigue, and potential for Torsades de Pointes (due to prolonged QT interval from K+ channel blockade).

Verapamil

Class IV Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Calcium channel blocker that blocks the slow Ca++ current.
  • Slows SAN firing rate and AVN conduction velocity.
  • Suppresses slow responses in cardiac tissue during ischemia and Digitalis toxicity.
Indications
  • Supraventricular tachycardia.
  • Used to reduce ventricular rate in atrial flutter and atrial fibrillation.
Side Effects
  • Bradycardia, AV block, hypotension, constipation, and peripheral edema.

Diltiazem

Class IV Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • Calcium channel blocker that blocks the slow Ca++ current.
  • Slows SAN firing rate and AVN conduction.
  • Suppresses slow responses in ischemia and Digitalis toxicity.
Indications
  • Supraventricular tachycardia.
  • Used to reduce ventricular rate in atrial flutter and atrial fibrillation.
Side Effects
  • Bradycardia, AV block, hypotension, dizziness, and flushing.

Adenosine

Miscellaneous Anti-Arrhythmic
Mechanism of Action
  • K+ channel opener producing hyperpolarization leading to Ca++ channel blockade, thereby slowing AVN conduction.
  • Dilates coronary and peripheral blood vessels.
  • Has a very short duration of action (about 15 seconds).
Indications
  • Drug of choice in paroxysmal Supra-ventricular tachycardia.
Side Effects
  • Bronchospasm.
  • Flushing and headache.
  • Chest pain.
  • Contraindications: Bronchial asthma and AV block.
  • Dosage note: Rapid bolus of 6mg (I.V) which may be repeated after 1-2 minutes.

Nitroglycerine

Nitrates / Organic Nitrates
Mechanism of Action
  • Denitration of the drug in smooth muscle cells releases NO (a vasodilator).
  • Nitrates bind to nitrate receptors in vascular endothelium and smooth muscle cells causing release of Nitric oxide. Nitric oxide stimulates guanylate cyclase (Soluble isoform), increasing levels of cGMP. This in turn causes dephosphorylation of myosin light chains, leading to reduction of Ca++ entry or promoting Ca++ exit and this leads to relaxation of smooth muscles (Vasodilation).
  • Reduces cardiac oxygen demand in classical angina (produces effect within 3 min in most patients).
Indications
  • Angina pectoris (Prevention and treatment of all types of Angina, including Classical, Variant, and Unstable angina)
  • C.H.F. (Congestive Heart Failure - reduces preload and relieves pulmonary congestion)
  • Acute Myocardial Infarction (may reduce the area of myocardial damage and preserve viable tissues)
  • Spasmolytic (to relieve pain due to spasm of urinary, biliary, and GIT)
  • Cyanide poisoning (Sodium nitrate I.V. may be used)
Side Effects
  • Flushing and throbbing headache
  • Postural hypotension, dizziness, and even syncope
  • Tachycardia, palpitation, and worsening of Angina
  • Tolerance (especially during chronic exposure)
  • Methemoglobinemia (may occur after chronic high doses)

Isosorbide Dinitrate

Nitrates / Organic Nitrates
Mechanism of Action
  • Denitration of the drug in smooth muscle cells releases NO (a vasodilator).
  • Nitrates bind to nitrate receptors in vascular endothelium and smooth muscle cells causing release of Nitric oxide. Nitric oxide stimulates guanylate cyclase, increasing levels of cGMP, leading to relaxation of smooth muscles (Vasodilation).
  • Effectively absorbed sublingually with a slower onset of action and longer duration of action compared to Nitroglycerine.
Indications
  • Angina pectoris (Prevention and treatment of all types of Angina)
  • C.H.F. (Congestive Heart Failure)
  • Acute Myocardial Infarction
  • Spasmolytic (urinary, biliary, and GIT spasm relief)
Side Effects
  • Flushing and throbbing headache
  • Postural hypotension, dizziness, and syncope
  • Tachycardia, palpitation, and worsening of Angina
  • Tolerance during chronic exposure

Isosorbide Mononitrate

Nitrates / Organic Nitrates
Mechanism of Action
  • Denitration of the drug in smooth muscle cells releases NO, stimulating guanylate cyclase and increasing cGMP to cause smooth muscle relaxation (Vasodilation).
  • Bypasses hepatic metabolism and has increased bioavailability (long-acting preparation).
Indications
  • Angina pectoris (Long-term prophylaxis and treatment)
  • C.H.F. (Congestive Heart Failure)
Side Effects
  • Flushing and throbbing headache
  • Postural hypotension and dizziness
  • Tachycardia and palpitations
  • Nitrate tolerance

Verapamil

Calcium Channel Blocker (Phenylalkylamine)
Mechanism of Action
  • Blocks L-type Ca2+ channels in smooth and cardiac muscle cells, reducing intracellular calcium.
  • Mainly affects the heart; slows atrioventricular (AV) conduction directly and blocks recovery of channels.
  • Decreases heart rate, myocardial contractility (greater negative inotropic effect than amlodipine), and pacemaker activity.
  • Dilates arterioles, decreasing total peripheral resistance, afterload, and myocardial oxygen demand. Has some alpha-adrenergic blocking activity.
Indications
  • Angina Pectoris (Angina of Effort, Variant Angina, and Unstable Angina in refractory cases)
  • Cardiac arrhythmias (Highly effective in PSVT and supraventricular arrhythmias)
  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (Salutary effect due to negative inotropic action)
  • Other uses: Suppressing migraine headaches and nocturnal leg cramps
Side Effects
  • Bradycardia
  • Headache and flushing
  • Dizziness and lethargy
  • Constipation
  • Risk of heart failure or heart block

Diltiazem

Calcium Channel Blocker (Benzothiazepine)
Mechanism of Action
  • Blocks L-type Ca2+ channels in smooth and cardiac muscle cells, reducing intracellular calcium.
  • Intermediate in its actions between verapamil (heart-selective) and nifedipine (vessel-selective).
  • Slows AV conduction, decreases the rate of firing of the sinus node pacemaker, and blocks recovery of channels.
  • Acts as a coronary artery vasodilator and relieves coronary artery spasm; less potent vasodilator than nifedipine and verapamil, with modest direct negative inotropic action.
Indications
  • Angina Pectoris (Angina of Effort, Unstable Angina, and particularly useful in Variant Angina)
  • Cardiac arrhythmias (Highly effective in PSVT and supraventricular arrhythmias)
Side Effects
  • Dizziness and lethargy
  • Headache and flushing
  • Risk of heart failure or heart block

Nifedipine

Calcium Channel Blocker (Dihydropyridine)
Mechanism of Action
  • Prototype DHP with a rapid onset and short duration of action (usually administered as an extended-release formulation).
  • Has a greater effect on smooth muscles of vessels than the heart itself; causes arteriolar dilatation, decreasing total peripheral resistance and afterload.
  • Improves myocardial oxygen balance by increasing coronary blood flow (vasodilation) and decreasing oxygen demand.
  • Causes direct depressant action on the heart only at higher doses.
Indications
  • Angina Pectoris (Angina of Effort, Variant Angina, and Unstable Angina in refractory cases)
  • Raynaud's phenomenon
  • Management of premature labour (alternative drug)
Side Effects
  • Reflex tachycardia and palpitations
  • Ankle edema (due to increased capillary pressure from arteriolar dilatation during chronic use)
  • Flushing, hypotension, headache, dizziness, drowsiness, and nausea
  • Paradoxically increased frequency of angina in some patients

Nicorandil

K+ Channel Opener / NO Donor
Mechanism of Action
  • Mediated through ATP-sensitive K+ channels, causing increased outflow of K+ ions from the higher intracellular concentration. This leads to hyperpolarization of vascular smooth muscles.
  • Also acts as a Nitric Oxide (NO) donor.
  • Combined mechanism results in both arteriolar and venodilator effects, with a prominent action of smooth muscle relaxation.
Indications
  • Angina pectoris (Newer antianginal therapy)
Side Effects
  • Flushing and palpitations
  • Weakness and headache
  • Dizziness and nausea
  • Vomiting
  • Painful aphthous ulcers in the mouth

Dipyridamole

Other Antianginal Drugs
Mechanism of Action

  • Acts as a powerful coronary dilator.
Indications
  • Management of Angina Pectoris
Side Effects

Indications

Side Effects
